# Academic talk examples

Use these distinctions when the report type changes the audience's question.
Adapt to the supplied purpose, audience and time; none mandates a slide order.

| Talk | Planning question | Evidence/content boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Paper-reading / lab meeting | What did the paper establish, how, and how does it bear on our question? | Separate the authors' claims, inspected evidence and the presenter's critique. Original figures and conditions should remain identifiable. |
| Own research / progress report | What question are we resolving, what changed, and what decision follows? | Distinguish completed results, current blockers and proposed work; retain unfavorable evidence and actual comparison scope. |
| Thesis defense | What coherent contribution is supported across the work? | Connect contributions to their evidence and limits; make assumptions and dependencies clear enough for examination. |
| Seminar / conference talk | What should this audience understand and remember? | Choose background and detail for their prior knowledge; retain the conditions needed for the main takeaway. |

An equation belongs when its meaning or assumptions are necessary to follow the
argument. A dense source figure can be introduced across slides while preserving
its scientific content and provenance. Put optional detail in backup slides when
that helps the requested talk. Source order need not become presentation order.

Timing follows explanation cost and transitions as well as slide count. A short
outline can record estimates; a claim that the talk was rehearsed needs an actual
rehearsal. Use audience questions and source evidence to judge the plan rather than
fixed slide counts, word caps or mandatory section quotas.
